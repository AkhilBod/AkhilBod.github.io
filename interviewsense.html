InterviewSense

An interview practice platform for CS students. You talk through a mock interview and get graded feedback on what was weak.

Most interview prep tools are question banks. You read a prompt, think of an answer, and nobody tells you that your answer rambled for four minutes before getting to the point. InterviewSense listens to you actually answer and then tells you what went wrong.

The core loop is simple. You pick a behavioral, technical, or system design round. The interviewer talks to you, you answer out loud, and when you're done you get a breakdown: structure, clarity, technical correctness, and the specific moments where the answer went sideways.

How it works

Speech goes through Deepgram for transcription and text-to-speech, so the interview feels like a conversation instead of a form. A fast model handles the live turns and a slower, stronger one does the grading after the session ends. Splitting it that way kept the conversation responsive without making the feedback shallow.

The coding round runs candidate code against test cases through Judge0, with a Monaco editor in the browser. For the topic and company question picks, I skip the model entirely and pull from a curated problem set, which turned out to be faster and more accurate than asking an LLM to pick a problem.

The part I am proudest of

Billing. That sounds boring, but it's where most of the real bugs lived. Every AI feature costs credits, and early on a failed model call could still eat a user's credit. Now a request reserves credits first, commits them only when the response finishes, and refunds them if anything fails or the stream is cut off. All of that happens inside database transactions, so two requests racing each other can't double-spend. Stripe webhooks are signature-checked and keep subscription state in sync, including older prices that grandfathered subscribers still pay.

Getting people to find it

Most of the traffic comes from search. A daily job pulls internship and full-time postings into structured data, and a content pipeline turns that into pages people actually search for. Unglamorous work, and it's where most of the users come from.

There's also a desktop companion app for macOS and Windows that signs in with a device code, keeps a rotating refresh token, and talks to the same credit system.
